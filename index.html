The cost of finding out late

What if the planning meeting started with "here's what changed — let's decide what to do," instead of the investigation into who missed what?

Every plan is built on assumptions about the outside world — commodity costs, customer demand, freight, monetary conditions. When those assumptions move, the P&L doesn't tell you for weeks. The signals that move first — prices, filings, earnings calls, shipping data — are already public. Most companies just aren't watching them until the quarter forces the question.

Same variance. Two different meetings.

Without the Signal-to-Plan Connection
"Diesel costs were up significantly — how and why did we miss that, and whose fault was it?"
With the Signal-to-Plan Connection
"Diesel is trending above our Q3 assumption and projected to hold. Here's the freight scenario at 10% above plan, and the levers we want to discuss."

The gap isn't access to data. It's the missing connection between external signals and the specific line items in your plan. Without it, planning meetings default to reconstructing what happened — often coupled with defensiveness and finger-pointing. With it, they start from what's already changing, and the conversation moves straight to what to do about it.

Here's how that connection gets built:

1
Detect
Continuous monitoring of a massive set of external signals — commodity markets, customer conditions, and macro data — including the specific ones we determine your plan actually depends on.
2
Connect
We map how external changes could impact your specific planning assumptions — so what you see is what the change means for your plan, not generic industry sentiment.
3
Surface
Delivered weekly, in your business context — including why a change may matter. When a signal crosses your materiality threshold, you see it sooner.
